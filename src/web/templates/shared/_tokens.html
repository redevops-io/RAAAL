{# The shared design system: tokens only, never layout. Two surfaces had independently chosen what "ok" looks like — #1f7a4d in the library, #4ba36b in the workspace — which is the same defect the visual semantics registry exists to prevent, expressed in CSS. A reader who learns that a colour means "clear" in one half of the product and then sees a different green in the other half has to learn it twice, and will eventually conclude the two mean different things. What is shared: typography, spacing scale, colour, status colour, chips, buttons, links, hierarchy. What is NOT shared: layout. A dense analytical surface and a decision surface should not resemble each other in structure — researchers expect a Bloomberg terminal, a person planning their savings does not. Sharing tokens is what makes them feel related; sharing layouts is what would make them both wrong for one of the two audiences. #}
